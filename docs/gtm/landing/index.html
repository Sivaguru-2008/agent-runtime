02 · the questions

Three questions an auditor will ask. Three answers a trace gives.

case/01

The Double Charge

“Show me the second refund — and why your system fired it.”

It didn't fire, and the trace proves it. Inside the fence, side effects execute exactly once. The retry is recorded arriving and being absorbed against the original effect's idempotency key. One refund, one effect, provably.

case/02

The Contested Decision

“Your agent declined this dispute. Which rule fired, under which configuration?”

The trace reconstructs the decision: exact input, the config version active at that moment, the rule that fired, the effect it caused. Replay it and the same decision falls out, identically. The match is the evidence.

case/03

One-off, or 500 More Times

“Is this an isolated decision, or is it in the queue right now?”

We scan recorded traces for the same input-and-rule pattern. You answer with a count: how many executions match, which are still pending, what they would do. A query over evidence, not a guess.

03 · the mechanism

Why this is proof, not telemetry

  1. 01

    Fenced execution

    Every agent action is appended to a cryptographically fenced event sequence. Events can't be inserted, deleted, or reordered after the fact. Side effects execute exactly once, even across retries and crashes.

  2. 02

    Deterministic replay

    Re-run any execution and get identical decisions. A replay that provably matches its original is not a re-telling. It's a demonstration.

  3. 03

    Forensic reconstruction

    For any decision: the input, the active config, the rule that fired, the side effect it caused. Then pattern detection — one-off, or about to happen 500 more times.

04 · the category

Observability shows what happened. Durable execution re-runs work. Proof does more.

Observability

Shows what happened.

Logs, metrics, traces — written by the same system that's under question, stored in mutable indexes. Built for debugging. It answers “where did it break?”

Durable execution

Re-runs work.

Retries workflows until they complete. Built for uptime. It answers “did it finish?” — and says nothing about whether the record can be trusted later.

Proof runloop

Shows what happened — and that it couldn't have been forged.

Fenced events, exactly-once effects, deterministic replay. Built for the audit. It answers “can you prove it?”

05 · the deadline

Traceability is no longer a preference

EU AI Act, Article 12 — high-risk AI systems must automatically record events over their lifetime, sufficient to trace the system's functioning.

US banking supervision — model-risk guidance is applied to AI systems in production, including the ability to reconstruct and validate individual decisions.

The question is coming. Only the answer is optional.
