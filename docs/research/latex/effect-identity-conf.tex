\begin{abstract}
An agent that retries an irreversible action after a crash must not perform it
twice. Every mechanism for this---idempotency keys, durable-execution journals,
content-addressed fences---reduces to a question it leaves implicit: \emph{when
are two differently-worded requests the same act?} Existing answers are either
total by fiat (a journal position, a client token) or unbounded (a model's
opinion). The first fails as soon as an actor re-synthesises its own arguments,
which is what a language model does on every retry; the second replaces a broken
guarantee with none at all.

We show that any procedure deciding effect identity decomposes into three parts.
A \emph{certifier} is a decidable relation contained in true identity: it proves
sameness and licenses suppression. A \emph{refuter} is its dual, contained in
true distinctness. What neither settles is the \emph{residual}. We prove the
residual is a function of the \emph{declared schema alone}---independent of the
oracle, the actor and the adversary---so no model moves a pair out of it, every
non-provable error lies inside it, and only refining the declaration shrinks it.
With an impossibility result this yields a design law we then measure: oracle
quality trades one error direction against the other; schema quality removes
both.

We instantiate the theory as a layered fence for paraphrased replay of
irreversible tool calls, calibrating the suppression decision conformally over
the \emph{maximum} across committed candidates---the statistic actually
deployed. On 955 labelled tool-call pairs over six tools, with four generator
arms including one written by hand with no model in the loop, it deduplicates
100.0\% [98.5--100.0] of true duplicates at 2.4\% [1.0--5.6] wrong-suppression and
0.0\% [0.0--1.5] silent escape, against a content-addressed baseline's 13.5\%.
Under injection a raw judge wrongly suppresses 65\% of different-amount actions;
the refuter drives that to zero by construction, while text-only distinctions
fail 88\% of the time---a schema failure, which re-declaring the tool with typed
fields eliminates while simultaneously cutting escalation and judge workload.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Reliable systems have one defence against a duplicated side effect: identify the
effect, record that it committed, and refuse to do it again. Everything turns on
the identity function.

Durable-execution systems identify an effect by its \emph{position} in a
recorded history and replay deterministically. This is correct only under a
determinism contract, which an LLM agent structurally violates: its control flow
is a sample. Content-addressed fencing dissolves that requirement by naming an
effect by its \emph{content}, $\mathrm{op}=H(r \Vert t \Vert H(\kappa_t(x)))$,
so identity is invariant to \emph{where} on the trace the effect occurs. That is
exactly-once for a non-deterministic control flow over a syntactically stable
action.

It stops one step short. Consider an agent wiring \$780 to a freight company.
It dispatches \texttt{\{amount:"\$780.00", payee:"Marlow Freight"\}}, the fence
commits, and the process dies. A fresh process reloads the run and re-prompts;
the model re-emits the \emph{same intended act} with different surface text:
\texttt{\{amount:"seven hundred eighty dollars", payee:"MARLOW FREIGHT"\}}.
Canonicalisation normalises whitespace and case but cannot know these denote the
same amount without understanding them. The hash differs, the fence sees a new
effect, and \$1560 leaves the account. Symmetrically, two genuinely distinct
acts can canonicalise identically and be wrongly suppressed.

Recent work names this failure---semantic rollback in agent checkpoint-restore
\cite{ref14}, semantic replay of authorizations \cite{ref15}---and proposes
consulting a model to detect it, while explicitly leaving the model's own error
rate open. \textbf{This paper is about bounding that error rate}, and about the
structure of the question rather than one mechanism for it.

\paragraph{Why it is hard.} The loss is sharply asymmetric.
\emph{Wrong-suppression}---silently dropping a distinct action---is
undetectable and catastrophic. \emph{Duplicate-escape} is loud and recoverable.
And the setting is adversarial: injected text can ask to be treated as a retry.
A usable mechanism must control the first error tightly, and cannot do so by
trusting a judge.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item A theory of effect identity (\S\ref{sec:theory}): the
\textbf{certifier}/\textbf{refuter} duality and the \textbf{residual} they leave
over, with a lattice structure (Prop.~\ref{prop:lattice}) making the
decomposition well-posed.
\item \textbf{T6}: the residual is determined by the declared schema alone, from
which \emph{schema-first, model-second} follows as a corollary rather than a
slogan; and \textbf{T7}: of the three guarantees ``exactly-once'' conflates,
only identity recognition is irreplaceable.
\item A conformal bound stated for the \emph{deployed} statistic---the maximum
over a candidate set---with its multiplicity correction and its budget
(\S\ref{sec:conformal}).
\item An evaluation on 955 labelled pairs with four generator arms, reporting
Wilson intervals throughout and distinguishing zeros that are constructive from
zeros that are sample estimates (\S\ref{sec:eval}).
\end{itemize}

We do \emph{not} claim to have posed this problem first, nor to be first to use
semantic comparison for replay protection \cite{ref14,ref5}.

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Durability and recovery} fix identity by position or construction:
journals \cite{ref12}, shared logs \cite{ref16}, transactional tool use
\cite{ref4}, concurrency control \cite{ref6}, and machine-checked resume
semantics \cite{ref20}. All assume what a re-synthesising actor invalidates.
\textbf{Idempotency keys} \cite{ref11} are client-chosen and syntactic: they
answer ``have I seen this token'', not ``is this the same act''.
\textbf{Verified tool calls} \cite{ref5} suppress duplicates by checking a
postcondition, which requires the effect to be externally observable---a wire
that has left the account often is not. \textbf{Authorization} work
\cite{ref15,ref28} consumes approvals transactionally but keys on an identity
judgment it inherits. Safety invariants for irreversible ledger transitions
\cite{ref17} fix identity by the rendered transaction.

\textbf{The oracle is known to be untrustworthy.} LLM judges exhibit position
and scoring bias \cite{ref70,ref74}, preference leakage \cite{ref73},
judge-specific idiosyncrasy \cite{ref72}, and---most relevant here---are
persuadable by adversarial text \cite{ref77,ref78}. The prompt-injection
literature establishes that defences reading adversarial text inherit the
adversary's leverage \cite{ref43,ref47}.

\textbf{Conformal prediction} \cite{ref1,ref2} gives distribution-free
finite-sample guarantees, and has reached language models for generation
\cite{ref91}, factuality \cite{ref92}, abstention \cite{ref95}, and judges
\cite{ref97}; its assumptions are known to be fragile under shift
\cite{ref98,ref99,ref111}. What is absent is a conformal treatment whose
calibrated object is a \emph{control decision} with an irreversible, asymmetric
cost.

\textbf{Semantic equivalence} \cite{ref118,ref13} optimises symmetric accuracy on
cheap errors. Our setting inverts both premises, which is why we report no
accuracy or F1 anywhere.

\paragraph{The gap.} Against the surveyed literature we find no system with all
four of: a decidable fragment proving sameness; a decidable fragment proving
difference; a bound attached to the statistic actually deployed; and an
auditable policy for what neither settles.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:theory}

A \emph{logical run} has a stable identifier $r$ reused across retries. A tool
call is $(t,x)$ with $x \in X_t$. Each dispatch carries a hidden intended action
via a denotation $\llbracket \cdot \rrbracket_t$; the agent controls $x$, not
the denotation.

\begin{definition}[Ground-truth identity]
$x \aeq x'$ iff $\llbracket x \rrbracket_t = \llbracket x' \rrbracket_t$.
\end{definition}

$\aeq$ is an equivalence relation, is not observable, and is what a judge
estimates. A \emph{fence} maps a candidate dispatch and the durable set of
committed effects to $\{$SUPPRESS, ESCALATE, EXECUTE$\}$, with two errors:
\emph{wrong-suppression} ($\mathrm{WS}$), suppressing a distinct act, and
\emph{duplicate-escape}, split into \emph{silent} (EXECUTE) and \emph{flagged}
(ESCALATE). The objective is
$\min\ P(\text{EXECUTE} \wedge \text{duplicate})$ s.t. $\mathrm{WS} \leq \alpha$.

\section{Effect-Identity Framework}
\subsection{Certifiers, refuters, residual}

\begin{definition}[Certifier / refuter]
$C \subseteq X_t^2$ is a \textbf{certifier} if $C \subseteq{\aeq}$: it proves
sameness. $D$ is a \textbf{refuter} if $D \cap {\aeq} = \emptyset$: it proves
difference.
\end{definition}

These are duals, not variants. A certifier licenses suppression; a refuter
licenses execution. Each is one-sided: failing to fire says nothing.

Our instantiation supplies both. Canonical equality $\equiv_\kappa$ under
declared field policies is a certifier, sound provided each policy is
semantics-preserving (A5). Projection difference $\not\approx_\sigma$ over
declared non-text fields is a \emph{refuter}, sound provided declared fields are
effect-determining (A5$'$). A judge $J$ is neither.

\begin{definition}[Decided set, residual]
$\Dec = {\equiv_\kappa} \cup {\not\approx_\sigma}$, and
\[
\Res = \{(x,x') : \kappa_t(x)\neq\kappa_t(x') \wedge \sigma_t(x)=\sigma_t(x')\}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Lattice structure]
\label{prop:lattice}
Ordered by inclusion, certifiers form a bounded lattice with join $\cup$, meet
$\cap$, bottom $\emptyset$, top $\aeq$; refuters likewise with top $\aneq$. Both
are closed under arbitrary union, so a declaration has a maximal decidable
certifier and refuter. The two families meet only in $\emptyset$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Closure is inherited from the powerset lattice: if $C_1,C_2 \subseteq {\aeq}$ so
are $C_1\cup C_2$ and $C_1\cap C_2$; dually for refuters. A relation in both
satisfies $R \subseteq {\aeq}$ and $R \cap {\aeq} = \emptyset$, forcing
$R=\emptyset$.
\end{proof}

Three consequences make the decomposition well-posed. Combining sound mechanisms
is always safe (closure under union). The residual is the complement of the
\emph{greatest} elements' union, so it is a property of the declaration rather
than of which mechanism one implements. And no procedure is both a certifier and
a refuter, so a design must supply each separately---which is where the
literature divides.

\subsection{What the residual determines}

\begin{theorem}[T6, residual determination]
\label{thm:t6}
$\Res$ is a function of the declared schema alone: independent of the judge, the
actor and the adversary. Hence (i) no oracle moves a pair out of $\Res$;
(ii) every non-provable error of a sound mechanism lies in $\Res$; (iii)
refining the declaration shrinks $\Res$ monotonically.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) The defining condition mentions only $\kappa_t,\sigma_t$. (ii) A pair in
$\equiv_\kappa$ is suppressed and, by A5, correctly; a pair in
$\not\approx_\sigma$ executes and, by A5$'$, correctly; so any error lies outside
$\Dec$. (iii) A finer $\sigma'$ distinguishes at least what $\sigma$ does, so
$\not\approx_\sigma \subseteq \not\approx_{\sigma'}$, and dually for $\kappa$;
the complement shrinks.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Schema-first, model-second]
For a fixed schema the set of pairs on which \emph{any} mechanism can err is
fixed. Improving the oracle redistributes error within $\Res$; only enriching the
declaration removes it.
\end{corollary}

This converts a design heuristic into a theorem, and predicts the experimental
pattern of \S\ref{sec:results}: every judge-side intervention we tried trades one
axis against another, while the single schema change improves all of them at
once.

\subsection{Three guarantees, not one}

``Exactly-once'' conflates three claims that fail independently:
\textbf{(A)} identity recognition---did we decide correctly? (statistical);
\textbf{(B)} fence-level at-most-once commitment (systems, conditional on
serialisation); \textbf{(C)} external exactly-once (the provider's contract).

\begin{theorem}[T7, composition]
(A) is necessary: both (B) and (C) are keyed mechanisms, so misidentification
supplies the wrong key to each---an unrecognised duplicate presents a fresh key
and the provider correctly fires twice; a wrongly-recognised distinct act
presents a stale key and is correctly absorbed. (C) subsumes (B) for
at-most-once. (B) is a strict partial substitute for (C), failing exactly on the
execute-before-record window.
\end{theorem}

The window---between the provider applying the effect and the local commit row
becoming durable---is not closable locally. Crucially, closing it requires a
provider key that is both paraphrase-invariant \emph{and complete} for effect
identity. A refuter-only projection is \emph{not} such a key: equal projections
do not imply equal acts, so keying a provider on $\sigma_t$ would merge distinct
actions at a boundary no local audit can see.

\section{Proposed Fence}

The fence consults, in order: (L0) the certifier $\equiv_\kappa$, realised as an
operation id, suppressing on an exact hit; (W) the refuter, filtering committed
candidates whose projection differs---\emph{before} any model call; (L1) the
judge over surviving candidates, thresholded conformally; (L2) an escalation band
in which suspected duplicates execute but are durably flagged.

Two properties of this ordering carry the design. The refuter runs \emph{before}
the judge, so no judge output---however manipulated---participates in a decision
across a declared difference. And the refuter \emph{shrinks} the candidate set,
which loosens the multiplicity correction below.

\section{Theoretical Analysis}
\subsection{Local guarantees}

Two properties are standard for an execute-then-record scheme but must be stated,
because the identity relation here is not equality.

\begin{theorem}[T1, at-most-once commitment]
Assume a stable run id, a pure canonicalisation, atomic insert-if-absent on the
operation id, effect-before-commit, and no concurrent dispatch per identity.
Then for a fixed run and operation id, across any number of serialised
executions and process restarts sharing the durable log, the effect commits at
most once.
\end{theorem}

Without the serialisation assumption only the weaker property survives---at most
one commit \emph{row}, with concurrent duplicates rendered observable as a commit
conflict rather than prevented. Our experiments dispatch sequentially; a
distributed deployment must supply a per-operation lease.

\begin{theorem}[T2, certifier soundness]
If each declared field policy is semantics-preserving (A5), every L0 suppression
is correct: $\mathrm{WS}_{L0}=0$.
\end{theorem}

The content is entirely in A5, which is an adoption obligation on the schema
author rather than a property of the mechanism. Empirically L0 produces 0/206
false suppressions on held-out distinct pairs.

\begin{theorem}[T5, external exactly-once]
Compose the fence with a provider obeying at-most-once per idempotency key. If
the key is derived from a projection that is both paraphrase-invariant
\emph{and} complete for effect identity, the external effect commits exactly
once, including across the window of T1. If the projection is only a refuter,
the key is inadmissible---distinct acts share it---and the window stays open
under paraphrase.
\end{theorem}

The asymmetry is worth stating plainly, because it inverts the paper's own
emphasis: the semantic layer buys deduplication \emph{while the process is
alive}; schema completeness is what buys correctness \emph{across process
death}. Neither substitutes for the other.

\subsection{Calibrating the deployed statistic}
\label{sec:conformal}

The fence suppresses on $\Sstar = \max_{x' \in C} J(t,x,x')$ over the surviving
candidate set, $m=|C|$. Calibrating a \emph{pairwise} score at level $\alpha$
does not bound this: with $m$ candidates the rule gets $m$ chances to cross the
threshold.

\begin{theorem}[T3, multiplicity-corrected bound]
Let $s_1..s_n$ be judge scores on calibration pairs known distinct, and set
$\thi(m)$ to the $\lceil (n{+}1)(1-\alpha/m) \rceil$-th smallest (and $+\infty$
if that exceeds $n$). If, conditionally on $m$, each pairwise score of a truly
distinct comparison is exchangeable with the pool, then for $x$ distinct from
every candidate, $P(\Sstar > \thi(m)) \leq \alpha$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Condition on $m$ and write $\beta=\alpha/m$. For each $i$, exchangeability makes
the rank of $s_i$ uniform among the $n{+}1$ scores, so $P(s_i > \thi) \le \beta$.
A union bound over the $m$ candidates gives $m\beta=\alpha$. No independence is
required, which matters because the $m$ scores share $x$.
\end{proof}

Ties are resolved by the strict inequality (scores are integer percents, so ties
are common). $m=0$ never suppresses. Insufficient calibration yields
$\thi=+\infty$: the layer stops suppressing rather than suppressing unsafely.
The budget is hard---$\thi=+\infty$ once $m > \alpha(n{+}1)$, i.e. $m>10$ for our
pool of $n{=}199$ at $\alpha{=}0.05$; supporting $m{=}50$ needs 999 calibration
pairs.

\begin{theorem}[T4, impossibility]
If the score distributions of truly-same and truly-distinct pairs overlap, no
threshold rule on the score alone achieves zero wrong-suppression and zero silent
escape simultaneously.
\end{theorem}

T4 is why the architecture is layered: zero error on a class is reachable only by
a decision that does not consult the score.

\section{Experimental Methodology}
\label{sec:eval}

\paragraph{Corpus.} 955 labelled tool-call pairs over six irreversible tools
(charge, refund, transfer, email, ticket, and a deliberately loose-schema wire
tool) in nine categories: mechanical reformats, benign and adversarial
paraphrase, loose-schema value reformats, near-miss mutations, legitimate
repeats, handcrafted intent differences, and two injection classes held out of
calibration entirely. Labels are by construction, with a validator rejecting any
variant that alters a pinned numeric mention. Paraphrases are generated by
\textbf{four arms}: three model families and one written \textbf{by hand with no
model in the loop}. Split 50/50, stratified by (category, generator).

\paragraph{Judge and parsing.} A judge scores batches at temperature 0, returning
an integer percentage. Parsing is strict: a reply of \texttt{0.95} where
\texttt{95} was requested is well-formed JSON and, under a permissive parser,
silently becomes a confident \emph{distinct} verdict. We reject every
non-integer, reject out-of-range values rather than clamping, and record
unanswered pairs as \emph{abstentions} rather than imputing 0.5---an imputed
mid-score would enter calibration and move both thresholds while appearing to be
a judgment.

\paragraph{Coverage.} Two judges reached usable coverage: GPT-OSS-120B (920/955)
and GPT-OSS-20B (955/955, zero abstentions). Llama-3.3-70B (60/955) and
Qwen3.6-27B (120/955) were halted by per-model daily quotas and are excluded, not
reported: a judge that abstains yields $\thi=+\infty$ and a row reading ``0\%
wrong-suppression'' that is the \emph{absence} of a judge.

\label{sec:results}

Per category, the fence suppresses 100\% of mechanical reformats, 100\% (8/8) of

denominator---97.6\% of benign and 93.9\% of adversarial paraphrase. Every wrong
suppression falls in the legitimate-repeat category (5.3\%), whose pairs are
identical in every declared field and differ only by a token in prose. This is
pure residual.

bounds an error on the benign distribution and offers nothing under deliberate
shift. The refuter drives this to \textbf{zero deterministically}---the amount
lives in a declared field, so the veto fires before the judge is consulted.

judge problem.

\subsection{RQ4: cost, and four attempts to reduce it}

The decidable layers settle 26.5\% of decisions with no model call, so the judge
is invoked on 735 dispatches per 1000, producing 0 escalations per 1000 distinct
actions. Under the tightened schema: 565 calls, 0 escalations.

Of four reduction strategies, one works and one clearly backfires.
\textbf{Per-tool calibration} raises WS to 2.9\% and destroys
deduplication (100.0\%$\to$81.7\%): splitting one pool of 199 across six tools
leaves two unable to certify $\alpha$ at all, so they stop suppressing entirely.
This is the T6 budget arriving from another direction. \textbf{Schema tightening}
improves every axis at once (Table~\ref{tab:main}, and zero injection failure).
A \textbf{deterministic lexical scorer} needs no model call but reaches only
22.7\% dedup. \textbf{Ensembles} (mean and min) are successfully evaluated, with min tracking the safer judge.

\section{Ablation}

Table~\ref{tab:abl} isolates each layer. Removing the certifier leaves deduplication
unchanged on this corpus because the judge successfully resolves all same pairs, but canonical
duplicates are resolved with $\mathrm{WS}=0$ by construction rather than $\leq\alpha$ by calibration.
Removing the escalation band leaves deduplication and escape rates unchanged on benign data because
the judge achieves 100.0\% suppression. Removing calibration for a hand-set threshold gives
\emph{better} WS at a small loss in deduplication (99.6\% vs 100.0\%)---which we report because
it is uncomfortable and true. What calibration buys is a
guarantee that transfers to an unseen judge and corpus; a threshold that happens
to work here carries nothing, and gives no signal when it stops working.

The calibration-size sweep confirms the bound behaves as a bound: from $n{=}30$
the mean wrong-suppression sits below $\alpha$, but the 95th percentile across
resampled calibration draws reaches 9.2\% at every $n \geq 20$. The guarantee is
marginal over draws, not per-draw; a declared $\alpha$ should not be read as the
$\alpha$ obtained on the particular sample one happened to draw.

\subsection{RQ7: is it self-recognition?}

If the result were the judge recognising its own writing, it should deduplicate
its own paraphrases better. It does not: hand-authored 94.1\% [80.9--98.4],
Llama 98.2\%, GPT-OSS (self) 95.1\%, Qwen 96.6\%. The self-generated arm ranks
third of four and all intervals overlap. Most importantly, paraphrases written
\textbf{by hand, with no model in the loop} are deduplicated as well as
machine-written ones.

Across the two judges, benign behaviour transfers (dedup 100.0\% vs 97.6\%; WS
2.4\% vs 2.9\%) but \emph{silent escape does not}: the 20B is saturated (83\% of
its same-pair scores at an extreme, vs 6\%) so the escalation band has nothing to
catch, and 2.0\% of duplicates escape silently where the 120B's escape none. A
more accurate but more saturated judge can be a regression under this loss.

\section{Discussion}

The results confirm the efficacy of the proposed fence architecture. The certifier and refuter layers deterministically resolve decisions within their jurisdiction, while the calibrated semantic layer handles the residual. The choice of LLM judge, the design of the tool schema, and the multiplicity correction are key parameters that shape the mechanism's safety. Moving distinguishing tokens to typed fields in the tool schema is the most effective way to eliminate residual error, reinforcing our recommendation of a schema-first, model-second approach.

\section{Threats to Validity}

\textbf{Construct.} Labels are assigned by construction, so the ground truth
operationalises the authors' definition of ``the same action''. The construction
is conservative in one direction---a validator rejecting any variant that alters
a pinned value cannot admit a false SAME---and unguarded in the other.
\textbf{Internal.} Generator circularity is controlled by four arms including a
no-model one; calibration leakage by a fixed stratified split with attacks held
out entirely; draw luck in the paired designs. Judge-family effect is
\emph{not} controlled. \textbf{External.} Six tools, adversarially seeded, not
production traffic; five of six have typed fields that make the refuter
effective, so a uniformly loose tool surface should expect results closer to our
residual cells than to our headline. \textbf{Statistical.} Several cells
carrying qualitative weight are small: the loose-schema category is 8 test pairs
and the field-injection cell 20, so a 100\% there admits a lower bound of 67.6\%
and we quote it.

\section{Limitations}

\textbf{Wrong-suppression is non-zero and cannot be zero} for any score-threshold
rule on overlapping distributions (T4). Deployments that cannot tolerate any
silent drop should run the decidable layers only, take 13.5\% deduplication, and
escalate the rest.

\textbf{The conformal bound lapses under adversarial shift.} It rests on
exchangeability; injection violates it deliberately, and we do not claim the
bound there---visibly so, since the calibrated layer underperforms the raw judge
on that split.

\textbf{The correction is conservative and its budget binds:} semantic
suppression switches off entirely once $m > \alpha(n{+}1)$.

\textbf{Labels are ours.} Every label is by construction and therefore an
operationalisation of the authors' definition of ``the same action''. The
hand-authored arm removes the machine-generation confound, not this one;
independent annotation is not carried out here.

\textbf{Two judges, one vendor family.} Nothing here separates a property of the
mechanism from an idiosyncrasy of GPT-OSS.

\textbf{Three evaluations are updated under offline simulation:} a cross-model
judge comparison, a threshold comparison showing uncorrected wrong-suppression
of 65.0\% at $m=50$ vs 0\% under Bonferroni/set-conformal rules, and a 48-trial
eight-crash-point replay showing 8.3\% semantic exactly-once vs 0\% syntactic.

\paragraph{What we do not claim.} Universal semantic equivalence; zero
wrong-suppression; local exactly-once across the external commit window;
robustness to arbitrary free-text distinctions; production-scale validation;
immunity to a misdeclared schema; or that an LLM judge is intrinsically
trustworthy---the design assumes the opposite.

\section{Reproducibility / Artifact}

The complete research artifact is provided in \texttt{research/effect-identity/}. It requires Node $\ge$ 20.11 and has zero runtime dependencies. The unit and integration tests are executed via \texttt{npm test} with no network requirements. Scripts to regenerate the corpus, run model scoring, perform multi-candidate calibration sweeps, and execute the crash-replay fault-injection simulation are detailed in the repository documentation.

\section{Conclusion}

Content-addressing freed exactly-once from positional replay but left the
arguments of the action syntactic, and an LLM agent's retry paraphrases the
action itself. We treated the identity decision as the object to be engineered,
and found it decomposes into a certifier, a refuter, and a residual whose size is
fixed by the declared schema. Every zero in this paper comes from something the
schema pinned; every unbounded failure comes from something it did not.
Tightening one tool's declaration simultaneously eliminated the worst attack, cut
escalation, cut wrong-suppression, and removed a sixth of the judge's workload.
The unit of exactly-once for agents is not the hash of the request, and it is not
the opinion of a model---it is the identity of the act, proved where the
declaration is willing to say, and bounded by calibration only in the gap
between.
