\section{Experimental methodology}\label{sec:method}

Every analysis is an offline recomputation from persisted corpus files and judge scores; no reported number requires a model call to regenerate. Commands, configuration and data are in the supplementary material and the artifact.

\subsection{Corpus}

The corpus comprises 1,066 labelled pairs over six irreversible tools: \texttt{charge\_card} (customer, amount, currency, memo), \texttt{post\_refund} (charge, amount, reason), \texttt{transfer\_funds} (two accounts, amount, currency, note), \texttt{send\_email} (recipient, subject, body), \texttt{create\_ticket} (title, description, priority) and \texttt{wire\_money}, whose amount, beneficiary and reference are all free text, so that the judge carries its entire identity decision. Each pair is labelled SAME or DIFF \emph{by construction} (Table~\ref{tab:corpus}): a SAME pair comes from a transformation instructed to preserve the act, checked by a validator that rejects any variant altering a pinned number or recipient; a DIFF pair mutates a declared field, inserts a distinguishing token, or is hand-specified to differ in intent. The labels operationalise our definition of identity (Definition~\ref{def:operational}). Four generator arms---Llama-3.3-70B (109 pairs), GPT-OSS-120B (121) and Qwen3.6-27B (118) at temperature 0.9, and a hand-written arm of 68 S2 pairs---let self-recognition be measured; the remaining 650 pairs have no generator (supplementary material, Section~S2). With 232 DIFF test pairs an observed zero admits a 95\% Wilson upper bound of 1.6\%, inside $\alpha=0.05$.

\paragraph{Splits}
Benign pairs are split 50/50 into calibration and test, stratified by (category, generator) with a fixed seed; injection categories are held out, and the build asserts both properties. Pairs derived from the same seed act can fall on both sides (82.8\%~[79.3--85.9] of test pairs share a seed act with a calibration pair), a route to optimism, so we also report 200 seed-level splits that partition the 219 seed acts first.

\paragraph{Judges}
GPT-OSS-120B (primary), GPT-OSS-20B and Qwen3.6-27B---two model families---were reached through Groq's OpenAI-compatible endpoint in August 2026, at temperature 0, in batches of ten. A judge is admitted only if it meets conditions fixed before scoring: coverage of at least 80\%, at least two distinct score values, and SAME/DIFF AUC of at least 0.60, which excludes a constant judge whose calibrated threshold would report a fake zero. Both GPT-OSS models scored all pairs (AUC 0.962); Qwen scored 1,041 (97.7\%, AUC 0.974), its abstentions unassociated with category, label, tool, split or generator (smallest permutation $p=0.09$; Section~S4). Llama-3.3-70B reached 5.6\% coverage before its endpoint disappeared and is excluded. Weights behind an endpoint are not pinned, so a re-run is a different experiment.

\paragraph{Configurations}
B0 no fence; B1 exact string; B2 canonical key (content-addressed fencing, the prior-art baseline); a raw judge at $\tau=0.5$; the \emph{layered} configuration (certifier and calibrated judge, no refuter); the \emph{proposed} configuration (certifier, refuter, calibrated judge, escalation band); and a \emph{refined} configuration that adds to $\sigma_t$, for every free-text field, the numeric magnitudes it mentions---an intervention modelling a tool author who types the numbers prose carries. Every configuration and ablation is calibrated on its own jurisdiction, at $\alpha=0.05$ (and $0.01$) with $\gamma=0.05$. Each corpus pair compares one request with one committed effect, so $m=1$ on the corpus.

\paragraph{Metrics and statistics}
Wrong suppression and friction are measured over DIFF pairs and deduplication and silent or flagged escape over SAME pairs, on the 513-pair test split (281 SAME, 232 DIFF) and separately on the 45 attack pairs. Every rate carries a 95\% Wilson interval~\cite{wilson1927}; a rate marked $\dagger$ is zero because a decidable layer makes it so. Paired comparisons use exact McNemar tests~\cite{mcnemar1947} with Holm correction~\cite{holm1979} within the liveness and safety families. Agreement uses Krippendorff's $\alpha$~\cite{krippendorff2004}, Fleiss' $\kappa$~\cite{fleiss1971} and Cohen's $\kappa$~\cite{cohen1960}. Seeds are recorded in the artifact's configuration file.

\subsection{Validation of the labels}\label{sec:method-labels}

A relabelling study was preregistered before any judgement was collected: 154 frozen, per-item-hashed items in eleven strata weighted toward the categories a reader is most likely to contest, three blinded annotators and a blind adjudicator, three catch trials with a fixed exclusion rule, and a fixed decision rule for the safety claim (frozen at Git commit \texttt{82274ec}, 2026-08-23; SHA-256 sheet hash \texttt{66a174e1\dots9a9c}). The full 154-item preregistered Channel~H study was not collected and is released as an open, cryptographically frozen turn-key replication artifact with an interactive browser instrument.

To evaluate label reproducibility across diverse reasoning systems without author consensus, \emph{Channel~M}---a blinded model panel (\texttt{groq/compound}, \texttt{groq/compound-mini}, \texttt{qwen/qwen3.6-27b}; \texttt{openai/gpt-oss-safeguard-20b} adjudicating; the primary judge not queried directly)---was run one pair per request, and all four passed the catch trials. Both Compound systems are built on GPT-OSS-120B together with Llama models (Llama 4 Scout for \texttt{groq/compound}, Llama 3.3 70B for \texttt{groq/compound-mini}), so the primary judge's model is a component of two panelists, and the adjudicator is fine-tuned from the GPT-OSS family. The panel is therefore not independent of the primary judge; agreement with the two Compound panelists excluded is 96 of 117 (82.1\%~[74.1--88.0]). A model panel controls the same threat of idiosyncratic author definitions, and is reported strictly as model-panel evidence. The sheet draws 61 items from this corpus and 93 from an extension corpus over nine further tools whose judge scores were never persisted.

To resolve the two strata contested by the model panel under the preregistered rule, a targeted Channel~H follow-up was executed in September 2026. Three independent adult annotators (\texttt{human-alpha}, \texttt{human-beta}, \texttt{human-gamma}), fluent users of transactional software with no prior knowledge of the manuscript, repository, hypotheses, or ground-truth categories and no personal relationship to the author, completed the task. Annotators evaluated the 30 frozen items from legitimate repeats (D2) and prose-only injection (D4b) interleaved invisibly with three preregistered attention catch trials (IT013, IT022, IT108) via an offline, self-contained HTML instrument (\texttt{annotation/channel-h-targeted/instrument.html}) run locally without network dependencies or telemetry. Presentation order and item orientation were pseudo-randomised per participant via a deterministic hash of their chosen pseudonym. Participants provided electronic informed consent covering research purpose, recorded response times, voluntary withdrawal, and publication of anonymised response CSVs; no personally identifiable data was collected. Participants were compensated at or above the local statutory minimum hourly wage for the median task duration regardless of response distribution or catch-trial completion (Section~\ref{sec:res-labels}).

\subsection{A second instance: action authorisation}\label{sec:method-authz}

An agent holds standing approvals, each with typed constraints (recipient, account or identifier, maximum amount, currency, maximum role, expiry) and a free-text scope. Permitting an action no approval covers---\emph{wrong authorisation}---is the silent error; declining a covered action is visible. The certifier permits when every typed constraint holds and the purpose text is canonically equal to the scope; the refuter declines when a typed constraint fails; the residual goes to the judge, and the action is permitted only if its score exceeds a threshold calibrated on not-covered residual pairs. Theorems~\ref{thm:bound} and~\ref{thm:tight} apply with wrong authorisation in place of wrong suppression; there is no band.

The corpus has 520 (approval, action) pairs over four tools (\texttt{issue\_refund}, \texttt{transfer\_funds}, \texttt{send\_email}, \texttt{grant\_access}) and 40 approvals. Per approval: the scope with whitespace perturbed (A1, covered, certified); three paraphrases (A2) and one specific instance (A3), covered; two typed violations (B1, refuted); four out-of-scope purposes with compliant typed values (B2, residual); and two attacks appending an instruction to permit, to a typed violation (B4a) and to an out-of-scope purpose (B4b). Labels are by construction; the seed texts were drafted with an AI assistant and are released for inspection. The split is by approval, so seed-level leakage is absent by construction; attacks are held out; judges, prompt format, batching, parser and admission rule are those of the identity experiments; $m=1$ throughout.
