\section{Limitations and broader impact}\label{sec:limitations}

Each caveat of the paper is stated here in full; elsewhere it appears at most as a clause.

\subsection{Limitations}

\paragraph{Labels and human validation}
Every label operationalises our definition of identity. Independent human validation was designed, preregistered and instrumented but not collected. The only evidence that the labels are shared by other readers is a blinded panel of language models, a weaker control: a notion of ``the same act'' shared by the author and the models would pass it and could still diverge from human practice. The panel contested two strata (legitimate repeats and prose-only injection), and its preregistered safety re-derivation triggered, in a computation with two defects that we report rather than correct away (Section~\ref{sec:res-labels}). A targeted human follow-up on the two contested strata is prepared but not run; the preregistered study remains uncollected. The safety conclusion was stable under the relabellings we could construct; the liveness conclusion was not.

\paragraph{One constructed corpus}
The corpus is synthetic and purpose-built, covers six tools, and says nothing about the distribution of paraphrase in production. Rates are properties of this corpus; what transfers are the decomposition, the bound and the dilemma. The authorisation corpus has author-drafted seeds and no independent annotation.

\paragraph{Calibration validity}
Theorem~\ref{thm:bound} needs exchangeability between the pool and the deployed comparisons, which cannot be verified at deployment and is violated deliberately by injection. The bound is marginal in two senses. Over calibration draws: removing seed-level sharing raised mean wrong suppression by a factor of about 1.5--2, and 10.5\% of seed-level splits exceeded $\alpha$ on the primary judge. Over kinds of difference: on the primary judge hand-specified semantic differences are wrongly suppressed at 6.5\%, above $\alpha$, and per-kind validity costs deduplication (Section~\ref{sec:res-class}). A realised rate can exceed $\alpha$ for a particular deployment.

\paragraph{Multiplicity}
The corpus has $m=1$. The correction is supported by simulation and by one live experiment with a weak, saturated judge. An experiment that builds multi-candidate units from the corpus and scores them with the primary judge, which would also measure response attribution at $m\ge2$ (Section~\ref{sec:problem}), is prepared and released but not run. The candidate set is every effect committed in the same run for the same tool; retries under a new run identifier, and the same act reached through a different tool, are missed.

\paragraph{Free text and injection}
Where distinctions are carried only by prose, the mechanism is only as good as its judge, and under injection it is worse: prose-only injection succeeded 84.0\% of the time on the primary judge. The refuter's protection does not extend to free text.

\paragraph{Schema and judge dependence}
The decidable layers are exactly as sound as the declarations they read (A5, A5$'$), and mis-declarations are not detected automatically. Deduplication, silent escape and injection robustness depend on the judge; two of the three judges share a family, and a third vendor was never reached. Models behind an endpoint change without notice, violating exchangeability until the pool is recalibrated.

\paragraph{External semantics, storage and concurrency}
The mechanism does not provide external exactly-once execution; a crash inside the commit window re-fires under paraphrase unless the provider supplies a key complete for identity, which no tool in the corpus has. The research log is not crash-atomic (A3 is partial), and concurrent dispatch (A7) was not tested.

\paragraph{Cost and the choice of $\alpha$}
The judge is consulted on 76.2\% of dispatches, at about 184 tokens per comparison in batches of ten. Stricter levels buy safety with liveness: at $\alpha=0.01$ the primary judge deduplicates 22.1\% and routes 64.8\% of duplicates to review.

\subsection{Ethics and broader impact}

The mechanism is meant to reduce harm from irreversible agent actions, and its two failure modes carry different ethical weight. Wrongly suppressing a legitimate action---a refund never issued, a message never sent---is silent, and the person affected may never learn of it; the design bounds this error but does not eliminate it. Executing an unintended duplicate is visible and recoverable, and the design deliberately tolerates more of it. Adversaries can target both directions; the refuter removes forced suppression whenever the distinction is typed, which is a reason to type act-determining fields rather than to trust a better judge. The injection templates are standard in the literature and released for defensive evaluation. Human oversight enters through the escalation band and audit; a band that emits more flags than reviewers can handle is not a safety mechanism~\cite{turan2026oversight}, so the fence records every semantic suppression with the matched effect, score, $m$ and threshold. We do not recommend the mechanism as the only safeguard for financial or safety-critical agent operations. The corpora are synthetic and contain no personal data.
