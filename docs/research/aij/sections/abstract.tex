An autonomous agent that retries an irreversible action re-synthesises its arguments, so two requests for the same real-world act may share no surface token. Content-addressed keys then miss the duplicate, and an unconstrained language-model judgement offers no bound on the opposite, silent error of suppressing a distinct act. We treat effect identity as a decision under asymmetric loss with a stochastic oracle and adversarial input. Any identity procedure decomposes into a certifier that proves sameness, a refuter that proves difference, and a residual that neither settles, determined by the declared tool schema alone. Our fence consults a language-model judge only on the residual and suppresses only when the maximum score over the $m$ surviving candidates exceeds a conformal threshold at level $\alpha/m$. Under exchangeability, wrong suppression is then bounded by $\alpha$, provided the calibration pool is drawn from the rule's own jurisdiction; a mismatched pool hides its own violation. Refining the schema, or demanding validity per kind of difference, shrinks the calibration budget: a refinement dilemma we state exactly. On 1,066 constructed pairs the fence deduplicates 94.7\% of paraphrased duplicates at 1.7\% wrong suppression, against 12.1\% for canonical keys, and wrong suppression stays below $\alpha=0.05$ for three judges while deduplication ranges from 82.6\% to 94.7\%. Typed fields eliminate field injection; prose-only injection succeeds 84\% of the time. The labels are constructed, and no human annotation of them was collected.
