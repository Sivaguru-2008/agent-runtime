\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Language-model agents now act through tools whose effects cannot be undone---charging cards, moving money, sending messages, creating records~\cite{yao2023react,schick2023toolformer,yao2024taubench}. When such an agent crashes, times out or is re-prompted, its recovery path re-issues work, and the surrounding system must not perform the same irreversible action twice. The classical defence gives each effect an identity, records it when the effect commits, and refuses a second commit under the same identity~\cite{birrell1984rpc,kleppmann2017ddia,stripe2026idempotency}. Journal positions in durable-execution engines~\cite{temporal2026docs,restate2026docs}, client-chosen idempotency keys~\cite{stripe2026idempotency} and hashes of a canonicalised request all answer one question implicitly: \emph{when do two requests denote the same act?}

For a deterministic program the answer is positional or syntactic, because a retried call replays the original byte for byte. An LLM agent breaks both assumptions: its control flow is sampled, and its \emph{arguments} are re-synthesised. An agent asked to wire \$780 to Marlow Freight for shipment 88213 may first emit
\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
wire\_money\{amount\_text: "\$780.00", beneficiary\_text: "Marlow Freight", reference: "Shipment 88213"\}
\end{quote}
and, after a crash and a re-prompt,
\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
wire\_money\{amount\_text: "seven hundred eighty dollars", beneficiary\_text: "MARLOW FREIGHT", reference: "shipment \#88213"\}
\end{quote}
The two canonicalise and hash differently, and a content-addressed fence wires the money twice. Conversely, two requests that differ in one token of free text (``instalment 1 of 2'' against ``instalment 2 of 2'') can be two legitimate acts that must both execute.

Deciding identity therefore requires interpreting arguments, and the two errors are sharply asymmetric. Executing a duplicate is costly but \emph{observable}: two committed records exist for reconciliation to find. Suppressing a distinct act is \emph{silent}: the fence returns the recorded response of an earlier act, the agent believes the new act happened, and no trace shows otherwise. We call this second error \emph{wrong suppression} and treat it as the safety-critical direction. Effect identity is thus a problem of reasoning under uncertainty with asymmetric loss, and an instance of a general one for autonomous agents: a control decision whose errors are irreversible and asymmetric, taken with a stochastic oracle as the only general-purpose decider, on inputs an adversary can write.

Canonical keys are sound but incomplete: they never merge two distinct acts and miss every paraphrased duplicate. Recent work identifies this failure in agent checkpoint--restore~\cite{zheng2026acrfence} and in authorisation~\cite{xu2026caplease}, and proposes asking an analyser model whether a re-synthesised call matches a logged one~\cite{zheng2026acrfence}. An LLM judgement is the only general-purpose checker for such predicates, but LLM judges are biased and promptable~\cite{zheng2023judge,wang2023fair,ye2024justice} and steered by adversarial text~\cite{greshake2023injection,hwang2025persuasion}. What is missing is a decision procedure whose safety-critical error is \emph{bounded} rather than inherited from an unconstrained judge. We ask whether semantic effect identity can be made such a procedure, and what the declared tool schema determines about how far that is possible.

\paragraph{Approach and results}
We decompose identity into a \emph{certifier}, a decidable relation that proves sameness (canonical equality under semantics-preserving field policies); a \emph{refuter}, a decidable relation that proves difference (inequality of declared typed fields); and the \emph{residual}, which neither settles and which alone reaches an LLM judge (Figures~\ref{fig:architecture} and~\ref{fig:flow}). The fence suppresses on the judge only when the maximum score over the $m$ candidates that survive the refuter exceeds a conformal threshold at level $\alpha/m$, calibrated on distinct pairs from the rule's own jurisdiction. The residual is a function of the declared schema and its typed-field policies alone, so every error of sound decidable layers lies in it (Proposition~\ref{prop:residual}). Wrong suppression is bounded by $\alpha$ for the deployed statistic under exchangeability---\emph{provided} the pool matches the jurisdiction; a wider pool lowers the threshold and hides the violation (Theorem~\ref{thm:bound}). The classically sharp union bound is attained by the deployed statistic, so the correction and the budget $\lfloor\alpha(n+1)\rfloor$ are forced (Theorem~\ref{thm:tight}), and refining the schema, or demanding validity within each kind of difference, shrinks that budget: the \emph{refinement dilemma} (Proposition~\ref{prop:dilemma}, Corollary~\ref{cor:mondrian}).

On the constructed evaluation corpus of 1,066 pairs over six tools, with three judges from two model families, the fence deduplicates 94.7\%~[91.4--96.7] of paraphrased duplicates at 1.7\%~[0.7--4.3] wrong suppression, against 12.1\%~[8.8--16.4] for canonical keys. Wrong suppression stays below $\alpha$ for all three judges (1.7\%, 1.3\%, 0.9\%) while deduplication ranges from 82.6\% to 94.7\% (89.0\% for the third). A pool drawn from outside the jurisdiction roughly doubles the residual rate while the whole-split rate stays under $\alpha$; in simulation an uncorrected threshold on the maximum of $m=50$ scores suppresses 89.0\% of distinct requests. The marginal bound hides a per-category rate above $\alpha$ on one judge, and per-category validity costs deduplication. The refuter removes a typed-field injection that the raw judge accepts 65.0\% of the time, but prose-only injection succeeds 84.0\% of the time; typing the numbers prose mentions closes that class at the price of deduplication (69.0\% on the primary judge, 12.1\% on the others). Under seed-level resplitting mean wrong suppression rises to 2.7\% and 10.5\% of splits exceed $\alpha$, and re-scoring the same pairs in new batches at temperature 0 doubles it on the residual (\mclMOnePersistedCount{} to \mclMOneFreshCount{} distinct pairs), so the realised rate of a single scoring run is itself a draw and a deployment should expect a rate near $\alpha$. Label generalisation was audited via a blinded model panel (Section~\ref{sec:res-labels}), and the mechanism provides bounded identity recognition and fence-level at-most-once commitment, not exactly-once execution at an external provider (Section~\ref{sec:guarantees}).

\paragraph{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}[(C1),leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item \emph{Problem formalisation.} Effect identity for irreversible LLM-agent actions is formalised as reasoning under uncertainty with asymmetric loss, distinguishing identity recognition (A), fence-level at-most-once commitment (B), and external exactly-once execution (C) (Section~\ref{sec:problem}).
\item \emph{Structural decomposition.} A certifier/refuter/residual decomposition separates exact decisions from ambiguous semantic cases, with the residual determined by the declared tool schema and its typed policies (Sections~\ref{sec:framework}--\ref{sec:fence}).
\item \emph{Validity mechanism.} The residual LLM judge is calibrated on the actual jurisdiction of the decision rule, with multiplicity correction for the deployed max-over-candidates statistic, showing that the standard Bonferroni bound is attained for scale-free deterministic rules (Section~\ref{sec:theory}, Section~\ref{sec:res-bound}).
\item \emph{Schema refinement dilemma.} Schema refinement removes cases from the judge's jurisdiction and can improve deterministic safety, but simultaneously shrinks the calibration population and therefore the statistical budget (Sections~\ref{sec:theory}, \ref{sec:res-class}, \ref{sec:res-refinement}).
\item \emph{Targeted boundary evaluation.} An empirical evaluation tests the framework's boundaries: paraphrased duplicate suppression, wrong suppression, candidate multiplicity, jurisdiction mismatch, schema refinement, adversarial text, crash/replay across process death, and cross-model transfer (Sections~\ref{sec:method}--\ref{sec:results}).
\end{enumerate}
The individual techniques---canonicalisation, typed validation, LLM judging, split-conformal calibration, the Bonferroni correction---are known; the contribution is the formal composition for semantic effect identity under irreversible retry semantics, the conditions under which the composition is valid, the schema-determined refinement trade-off, and the targeted boundary evaluation.
