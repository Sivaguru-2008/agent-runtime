An agent that retries an irreversible action re-synthesises its arguments, so two requests for the same real-world act may share no surface token: content-addressed keys miss the duplicate, and an unconstrained LLM judgement gives no bound on the opposite, silent error of suppressing a distinct act. We treat effect identity as a decision under asymmetric loss. Any identity procedure splits into a certifier that proves sameness, a refuter that proves difference, and a residual that neither settles and that the declared tool schema alone determines. Our fence consults an LLM judge only on the residual and suppresses only when the maximum score over the $m$ surviving candidates exceeds a conformal threshold at level $\alpha/m$. Under exchangeability this bounds wrong suppression by $\alpha$, but only if the calibration pool comes from the rule's own jurisdiction: a mismatched pool hides its own violation. Refining the schema, or demanding validity per kind of difference, shrinks the calibration budget. On 1,066 constructed pairs the fence deduplicates 94.7\% of paraphrased duplicates at 1.7\% wrong suppression, against 12.1\% for canonical keys, and stays below $\alpha=0.05$ for three judges. We also report where the guarantee is fragile: prose-only injection succeeds 84\% of the time, and the judge's scores change with batch context at temperature 0, so calibration must share the deployment's scoring context. Labels are constructed and were checked only by a panel of language models.
