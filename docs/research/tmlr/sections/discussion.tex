\section{Discussion and limitations}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the evidence supports}
The most robust finding is an asymmetry between the error directions. Wrong suppression stayed below $\alpha$ across three judges, three labellings and the mean over 200 seed-level splits, while deduplication moved from 94.7\% to 82.6\% across judges, to 27.6\% across labellings, and to 69.0\% or 12.1\% under refinement. That is what a bound on one direction and none on the other should look like, not evidence of greater accuracy: at $m=1$ on judges this good, a well-chosen fixed threshold performs about as well on this corpus. What the calibrated rule adds is a threshold chosen without test labels, a correction that remains valid with several committed candidates, and a failure mode---no semantic suppression---that is safe when calibration data run out. The second finding is that the schema, not the judge, decides where the mechanism can be trusted: every benign wrong suppression and nearly every panel disagreement fell on a pair distinguished only in free text, and the one intervention that removed an attack class was a declaration change, paid for in deduplication through a smaller and harder calibration pool.

\paragraph{Practical guidance}
Declare every act-determining field as typed where possible. Calibrate each configuration on its own jurisdiction with the same judge, prompt and scoring procedure as deployment, and recalibrate when a layer, the judge or the schema changes. Because a single scoring run's realised rate is itself a draw, plan for a residual wrong-suppression rate near $\alpha$, not near the best value observed in validation. Size the pool for the candidate budget ($m/\alpha-1$ distinct residual pairs for $m$ candidates; 19 at $m=1$, per class if a per-class guarantee is needed). Log and sample semantic suppressions for audit, since the bound is marginal and a wrong suppression leaves no other trace.

\paragraph{Limitations}
\emph{Labels.} Labels are constructed and operationalise our definition of identity. No human annotation was collected; the only independent check is a model panel that is not independent of the primary judge, contested two strata, and whose preregistered safety test triggered. A targeted human study on the contested strata is prepared and released but not run.
\emph{Corpus.} The corpora are synthetic, cover six tools, and say nothing about how often retries paraphrase their arguments in production; the rates are properties of these corpora.
\emph{Statistics.} A6 cannot be verified at deployment and fails under injection and model drift; the bound is marginal over calibration draws (10.5\% of seed-level splits exceed $\alpha$) and over kinds of difference (D3 at 6.5\% on the primary judge); and the judge is noisy at temperature 0, so the realised rate varies between scoring runs of the same pairs (\stabMPWSCount{} to \stabMTWSCount{} distinct residual pairs; Section~\ref{sec:res-stability}), with our headline run at the favourable end.
\emph{Multiplicity.} The corpus has $m=1$; the correction's benefit is shown in simulation and with a saturated judge, and our corpus-derived candidate sets measure only its cost.
\emph{Adversaries.} Prose-only injection succeeded 84.0\% of the time on the primary judge; no bound is claimed under injection.
\emph{Systems.} We provide identity recognition and fence-level at-most-once commitment, never external exactly-once execution; the research log is not crash-atomic and concurrent dispatch was not tested. Two of three judges share a model family, all were reached through one provider, and the judge is consulted on 76.2\% of dispatches at about 184 tokens per comparison.

\paragraph{Broader impact}
The mechanism aims to reduce harm from irreversible agent actions, and its failure modes carry different weight: a wrongly suppressed refund or message is silent and may never be noticed, whereas a duplicate is visible and recoverable. The design bounds the first, marginally and under stated assumptions, and deliberately tolerates more of the second. It should not be the only safeguard for financial or safety-critical agent operations. The injection templates are standard and released for defensive evaluation; the corpora are synthetic and contain no personal data.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Every mechanism that stops an agent from repeating an irreversible action rests on deciding whether two requests denote the same act, and an agent that re-synthesises its arguments breaks the syntactic answers. Splitting that decision into a certifier, a refuter and a schema-determined residual, and calibrating the deployed max-over-candidates statistic on the residual, bounds the silent error---but only under conditions that are easy to violate: the pool must come from the rule's own jurisdiction, the guarantee is an average over calibration draws, over the judge's own scoring noise and over the kinds of difference the pool mixes, and refining the schema or the calibration population spends the same budget. On a constructed corpus the fence recovered 94.7\% of paraphrased duplicates at 1.7\% wrong suppression, against 12.1\% for canonical keys. The natural next steps are real multi-candidate agent traces, human validation of the contested strata, and monitoring protocols that track the realised rate of a noisy judge in deployment.
