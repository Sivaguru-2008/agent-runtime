\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Language-model agents act through tools whose effects cannot be undone: charging cards, moving money, sending messages \citep{yao2023react,schick2023toolformer,yao2024taubench}. When an agent crashes, times out or is re-prompted, its recovery path re-issues work, and the surrounding system must not perform the same irreversible action twice. The classical defence gives each effect an identity and refuses a second commit under the same identity: journal positions in durable-execution engines \citep{temporal2026docs,restate2026docs}, client-chosen idempotency keys \citep{stripe2026idempotency}, or hashes of a canonicalised request \citep{kleppmann2017ddia}. Each answers one question implicitly: \emph{when do two requests denote the same act?}

For a deterministic program the answer is positional or syntactic, because a retry replays the original byte for byte. An LLM agent re-synthesises its arguments. Asked to wire \$780 to Marlow Freight for shipment 88213, it may first emit \texttt{\{amount\_text: "\$780.00", beneficiary\_text: "Marlow Freight", reference: "Shipment 88213"\}} and, after a crash, \texttt{\{amount\_text: "seven hundred eighty dollars", beneficiary\_text: "MARLOW FREIGHT", reference: "shipment \#88213"\}}. The two hash differently and a content-addressed fence wires the money twice. Conversely, requests that differ in one token of free text (``instalment 1 of 2'' and ``2 of 2'') can be two legitimate acts.

Deciding identity therefore requires interpreting arguments, and the two errors are asymmetric. Executing a duplicate is costly but \emph{observable}: two committed records exist for reconciliation to find. Suppressing a distinct act is \emph{silent}: the fence returns an earlier act's recorded response and no trace shows the loss. We call this \emph{wrong suppression} and treat it as the safety-critical direction. The only general-purpose checker for such a predicate is an LLM judge, and LLM judges are biased, promptable and steerable by adversarial text \citep{zheng2023judge,wang2023fair,greshake2023injection}. Recent work names the failure in agent checkpoint--restore and authorisation and proposes asking an analyser model whether a call matches a logged one \citep{zheng2026acrfence,xu2026caplease}; what is missing is a procedure whose safety-critical error is \emph{bounded}.

\paragraph{Approach}
We split identity into a \emph{certifier}, a decidable relation that proves sameness (canonical equality under semantics-preserving field policies); a \emph{refuter}, a decidable relation that proves difference (inequality of declared typed fields); and a \emph{residual} that neither settles and that alone reaches the judge (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}). The fence suppresses on the judge only when the maximum score over the $m$ committed candidates that survive the refuter exceeds a one-sided conformal threshold at level $\alpha/m$, calibrated on distinct pairs from the rule's own jurisdiction.

\paragraph{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}[(C1),leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
\item A formulation of effect identity as a decision under asymmetric loss, separating identity recognition from fence-level at-most-once commitment and from external exactly-once execution (Section~\ref{sec:problem}).
\item The certifier/refuter/residual decomposition: every error of sound decidable layers lies in a residual fixed by the schema, and only the residual needs the stochastic oracle (Proposition~\ref{prop:residual}).
\item Jurisdiction matching as a necessary condition: a multiplicity-corrected conformal bound for the deployed max-over-candidates statistic (Theorem~\ref{thm:bound}), the observation that the classical bound is exactly attained by that statistic (Theorem~\ref{thm:tight}), and a demonstration that a pool drawn outside the jurisdiction violates the bound while the whole-split rate stays below $\alpha$.
\item The refinement dilemma: refining the schema, or demanding validity per kind of difference, shrinks the calibration budget (Proposition~\ref{prop:dilemma}, Corollary~\ref{cor:mondrian}); both are measured.
\item An evaluation on 1,066 constructed pairs with three judges from two model families, covering injection, schema refinement, class-conditional validity, multiple candidates, scoring-context stability, relabelling and resplitting, with crash-and-replay and a second decision instance (action authorisation) in the appendix. Every quoted interval is regenerated and checked against the stored results.
\end{enumerate}
The individual techniques---canonicalisation, typed validation, LLM judging, split-conformal calibration, the Bonferroni correction---are known. The contribution is the decomposition, the conditions under which the composition is valid, and a candid evaluation of where it holds and where it does not.
